FinanceTracker keeps a history of what you own. You describe your accounts once, then
record what each one is worth on a date. Every number in the app — the dashboard, the
performance figures, the charts, what the assistant reads — is computed from those
recorded values. Nothing is estimated, imported from a bank, or inferred.

It is a native macOS app. There is one user, one database file, and no account.

:::note[Alpha]
The app is pre-1.0. Features move between releases and a bad import can leave your data
in a state you did not want. Export a backup from **Settings → Data & Backup** before
anything that matters, and keep a copy of the JSON somewhere else.
:::

It was built for one person's use case and has not been widened since:
[why I built this](/start/why/).

## The five things you will use

| Concept | What it represents | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Institution** | Who holds the money | UBS, Swissquote, PostFinance |
| **Category** | What kind of money it is | Savings, Investments, Pension |
| **Asset** | One account or position | "Global Equity Fund" at UBS, in CHF |
| **Snapshot** | The value of one asset on one date | 63,484.00 CHF on 2026-11-01 |
| **Tag** | A free label you invent | `retirement`, `liquid`, `swiss` |

An asset belongs to exactly one institution and one category, and has one currency.
A snapshot belongs to one asset and one date. That is the whole schema in spirit; the
rest is presentation.

## The rule everything depends on

**The newest snapshot of an asset is its current value.** Not an average, not a
projection — the last value you typed. An asset with no snapshot contributes nothing to
the portfolio value, and an asset you stopped updating keeps its last value forever.

That is why the dashboard has a **Days Since Update** tile: it is the only signal that
the numbers might be stale.

## What it deliberately does not do

- **No bank connections.** Nothing logs into your accounts, and no aggregator sees your
  credentials.
- **No transactions or budgets.** It tracks balances over time, not spending. The AI
  assistant can discuss a budget, but the app does not store one.
- **No multi-user or sync.** The database is a local file. Two Macs mean two databases
  and a manual export/import.
- **No cloud.** Three requests can leave the Mac, all of them yours to trigger: the
  optional European Central Bank rate fetch, the AI provider you configure, and the update
  check you press. [The complete list](/data/privacy/#the-complete-list-of-outbound-requests).

## What you get in return

- Multi-currency totals with rates you control, and a visible warning when a conversion
  is missing instead of a silently wrong total.
- Performance over a period, including the honest caveat that a deposit is not a gain.
- Composition of a fund by ISIN, when you want it, without turning the app into a
  portfolio accounting system.
- An assistant that reads your figures and can *propose* a snapshot or a memory, but
  cannot write anything without a confirmation click.
