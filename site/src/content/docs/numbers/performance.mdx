Performance compares the portfolio at the start of a period with the portfolio now. It is
one measure across all assets, not a per-asset return.

**A deposit is counted as performance.** The baseline is a value, not a list of
contributions, so adding money inside the period reads as growth. That is the caveat to
carry into every number on this page; the rest explains each figure and the case that shows
`N/A`.

## The period

Six buttons in the header:

`1 month` · `3 months` · `6 months` · `1 year` · `YTD` · `All time`

- The fixed ranges are counted back from **today**, not from your last snapshot. If you have
  not recorded anything for three months, a "1 month" window can start after all your data.
- **YTD** starts on 1 January of the current year.
- **All time** starts at the oldest snapshot in the database.

The start and end dates are printed under the title as `YYYY-MM-DD — YYYY-MM-DD`.

## The four figures

| Figure | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Total portfolio value** | What the portfolio is worth at the end | Each asset's newest snapshot on or before today, converted and summed |
| **Net performance** | Growth on the capital you held at the start | `(end − start) / start × 100`, two decimals, signed |
| **Absolute performance** | The same thing in money | `end − start`, base currency |
| **Annualised performance** | Growth per year over the period | `(end / start) ^ (1 / years) − 1`, where `years = days / 365.25` |

"Start" is **invested capital**: each asset's newest snapshot at or before the first day of
the period, converted and summed.

`N/A` appears instead of a percentage when the start value is zero or negative, when the
period has no length, or when the start date is today. It is not an error; there is simply
nothing to divide by.

## What counts as a gain — and what does not

The deposit caveat above is the whole limitation, and it has two shapes:

- A deposit inside the period raises Net performance as though the market did it.
- A new asset added mid-period has no snapshot at the start, so its entire value appears as
  growth. This is why the figure is labelled *"On the capital held at
the start"* rather than "return".

There is no cash-flow adjustment, no time-weighted return and no benchmark. For a
single-person net worth tracker that is a deliberate trade-off; for comparing against an
index it is not the right tool.

## The chart

**Portfolio Evolution** plots the running total across the period.

- Assets whose last snapshot predates the period are carried in at their last value, so a
  stale account still appears in the line.
- The final point is set to the summary total, so the line and the **Total portfolio value**
  tile always agree — even if that means the last plotted date is not the date of your last
  snapshot.
- The range label sits in the card header.

An empty period shows *"No snapshots in this period."* rather than a flat line at zero.

## The period card

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Period length** | Days between the start and end dates |
| **Invested capital** | The start value, in the base currency |

## The assets card

Two figures come from **system roles**, not from category names:

- **Cash** — total of assets in categories with the `cash` role.
- **Emergency fund** — total of assets in categories with the `emergency_fund` role.

**Show breakdown** expands one row per role present, in the base currency. Unclassified
categories fall into **Other**.

A `0.00` next to Cash means either that you have no cash or that no category is classified
as cash. Check **Categories** before concluding the first.

## How to make the number meaningful

- Record snapshots on the same day each month.
- Do not add assets mid-period if you want to read Net performance as a return.
- If you must compare across a change in exchange rates, remember that rates are not
  historical: editing one rewrites the past too.
